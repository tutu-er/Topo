Abstract. Customer power and voltage measurements can admit several low-voltage feeder trees with similar fits. We ask which groups of meters share a line across these alternatives. A power-balance gate selects the load model. If the model includes an unmetered load, its balance estimate enters the fit and its supports must form a nested path. We score each tree by its weighted voltage residual and a penalty per selected line. Nine tree builders supply candidates and feasible starting trees. For each grouping of a reference tree, a mixed-integer quadratic program (MIQP) searches the current pool for a tree that reverses that grouping. A valid exclusion above the reference score plus a fixed margin establishes agreement, a feasible rival within the margin exhibits disagreement, and an inconclusive search leaves the grouping unsettled. The search records its domain and bounds, revisits anchors as candidates accumulate, and checks compatible single-line additions. A deterministic signal-separation condition explains when these statements recover true groupings. A small exhaustive audit compares the search pool with all admissible supports on four inputs. A separate one-hour probing rule ranks feasible demand-response blocks by predicted separation of open alternatives and purchase cost.

I. Model

A feeder has a root, the point of common coupling (PCC), and \(n\) metered customers. Meter \(i\) reports \(p_{i,t}\), \(q_{i,t}\), \(V_{i,t}\); the PCC reports \(V_{0,t}\) and, where metered, \(P^{\rm pcc}_t\), \(Q^{\rm pcc}_t\). Every line feeds a set of leaves, its support; the supports of a tree are laminar (any two are nested or disjoint), and a laminar family containing \(\Bset=\{\{1\},\dots,\{n\},\Nset^+\}\) fixes the tree up to internal nodes without leaves, which voltages cannot see. A topology is a laminar family \(F\supseteq\Bset\) over \(\Nset^+\) with lines \(\Lin(F)=F\setminus\Bset\). With the linearised DistFlow equations,

\[ y_t=\sum_{C\in F} z_C\big[r_C\,(z_C\tp p_t+h_C u^P_t)+x_C\,(z_C\tp q_t+h_C u^Q_t)\big]+\varepsilon_{0,t}\one+o+e_t \]
(1)

with \(z_C\in\{0,1\}^n\) the meters of \(C\), \(h_C=1\) if \(s\in C\), \(0\le r_C,x_C\le\bar\theta\) twice the line's resistance and reactance in pu (the implementation uses \(\bar\theta=2\)), \(\varepsilon_{0,t}\) the PCC voltage error, \(o\) fixed meter offsets and \(e_t\) meter noise. One unmetered load is allowed; its power is the PCC balance minus the losses estimated from the meters' own voltages,

\[ u^P_t=P^{\rm pcc}_t-\sum_i p_{i,t}\Big(1+\frac{V_{0,t}-V_{i,t}}{V_{i,t}}\Big),\qquad u^Q_t\ \text{likewise}. \]
(2)

When the gate of Section II finds a load, it is one more leaf \(s\), with the estimated power profile (2) fixed during the fit and no voltage reading; otherwise there is no leaf \(s\). The supports that carry the load all contain \(s\), so in a laminar family they are nested: they are the load's path (Proposition 3). The load can sit below any line, below the trunk, or at the PCC (beside the trunk; the meters' trunk is then the line \(\Nset\) without \(s\)). Its own branch \(\{s\}\) affects no voltage: its columns are zero and it carries no price. A line is supported by how the loads on one side of its branching point move the voltages on the other side; a meter's own cable has no such branching evidence, so only lines with at least two leaves are topology decisions. The data come from AC power flow; (1) is used inside the method only. Assumptions stated, not modelled: the loss estimate is approximate, several unmetered loads are not one aggregate, and errors in the measured \(p,q\) enter the regressors.

II. Is there an unmetered load?

The PCC balance sets the load model before the tree search.

\[ \varsigma=\frac{\sum_tu^P_t}{\sum_t\sum_ip_{i,t}},\qquad\text{a load is modelled if and only if }\varsigma\ge\varsigma_0=1\%. \]
(3)

The 1% gate is a modelling choice calibrated on the 288 historical development cases in Table 1. With a near-zero estimated load profile, lines that differ only in carrying that load produce nearly identical voltage predictions. In these cases the no-load spread reflects meter gain errors (0.3% at the meters, 0.2% at the PCC) and the loss approximation. Other meter classes need their own calibration.

III. Score and labels

Score. The meter offsets \(o\) are unrestricted constants, removed by \(D=I_T-\Pi_{[\one]}\) (subtracting each meter's time mean). The PCC error is random and shared by all meters in a slot: with variance \(\sigma_0^2\) against \(\sigma_e^2\) for a meter, a slot's error covariance is \(\Sigma=\sigma_e^2(I_n+\rho\one\one\tp)\), \(\rho=\sigma_0^2/\sigma_e^2\), taken as the ratio of the means of \(V_0^4\) and \(V^4\). The weighting \(S_\gamma=I_n-\gamma\one\one\tp/n\) with \(\gamma=1-(1+n\rho)^{-1/2}\) satisfies \(S_\gamma\tp S_\gamma=\sigma_e^2\Sigma^{-1}\) (generalised least squares), and \(M=D\otimes S_\gamma\). The score is

\[ H(F)=\min_{\theta}\frac{\|M(y-\Phi_F\theta)\|^2}{2\hat\sigma^2}+\beta\,k(F),\qquad\beta=\tfrac12p_s\ln N-\lambda \]
(4)

Here \(N=Tn\) counts meter readings and \(k(F)\) counts optional lines. The implementation uses a feeder-wide rule \(p_s\in\{1,2\}\) based on how independently active and reactive powers vary across meters. The resulting penalty is shared by all candidate lines. Noise variance. \(\hat\sigma^2\) is the residual variance after applying \(M\) to the saturated model, in which each meter has its own coefficients for every leaf's flow,

\[ y_{i,t}=\sum_{j\in\Nset}\big(R_{ij}\,p_{j,t}+X_{ij}\,q_{j,t}\big)+R_{is}\,u^P_t+X_{is}\,u^Q_t+\varepsilon_{0,t}+o_i+e_{i,t},\qquad R=R\tp,\ X=X\tp\ \text{over the meters} \]
(5)

with every coefficient free and the load's two terms only when a load is modelled. Every tree's prediction has this form, and with the load's terms free \(\hat\sigma^2\) does not depend on the load's place. Its degrees of freedom are the rank of \(M\) minus the numerical rank of the saturated design. It is computed once, at set-up, like the starting trees (Appendix B); the score, the searches, the labels and probing keep the load as a leaf with its estimated injection held fixed. The working covariance accounts for the shared PCC error. Other correlations can affect the fitted scores.

Proposition 1 (branching penalty). For a tree with a fixed number of leaves, define the degree weight \(w(F)=\prod_v a^{c_v-2}\), where \(c_v\) counts the children of internal node \(v\) and \(0\lt a\lt1\). Then \(-\ln w(F)=\mathrm{const}-\lambda k(F)\) with \(\lambda=\ln(1/a)\).

The empirical branching weight \(a=0.141\) gives \(\lambda=1.96\), reducing the cost of adding a line. It was derived from 84 LV feeders also used to set the case generator's statistics. A comparison with \(\lambda=0\) measures sensitivity to this choice. The grouping margin \(m=4.6\) is a fixed difference in score units.

Groupings. With a load, one grouping of meters can appear as two supports, \(M\) and \(M\cup\{s\}\): the same statement about the meters, with the load inside the line or not. Write \(\pi(C)=C\cap\Nset\) and call \(\Gset(F)=\{\pi(C):C\in F,\ 2\le|\pi(C)|\le n-1\}\) the groupings of \(F\). Labels, anchors and probing concern groupings; the load's place is reported separately. Without a load, groupings are lines.

Labels. Fix the measurements, load model, score and current cumulative candidate pool \(\Omega\). Write \(H^*=\min_F H(F)\) over its laminar trees, with the base lines \(\Bset\) included and no anchors required. Its competitive set is \(\Sset_m=\{F:H(F)\le H^*+m\}\). For a feasible reference tree \(T\), the operational threshold is \(\tau=H(T)+m\). The flip problem finds the best tree in this domain that reverses membership of \(M\),

\[ H^{\rm f}_M(T)=\min\{H(F):\ F\subseteq\Omega\cup\Bset\ \text{laminar},\ [M\in\Gset(F)]\neq[M\in\Gset(T)]\} \]
(6)

which for \(M\in\Gset(T)\) is the tree MIQP (9) with every support of grouping \(M\) removed from the pool, and for \(M\notin\Gset(T)\) the same MIQP with a support of grouping \(M\) required. Its gap is

\[ e_M(T)=H^{\rm f}_M(T)-H(T)\ \in\ \big[\,L_M-H(T),\ U_M-H(T)\,\big] \]
(7)

Here \(L_M\) bounds the minimum of the original flip problem from below. A feasible flipped tree \(F_M\), called a rival, supplies the upper bound \(U_M=H(F_M)\). An unavailable endpoint stays missing. The code solves in units \(\mathrm{crit}=2H/N\) and converts the reported flip gaps to \(H\) units.

Definition 1 (reference-threshold labels). A grouping is decided if all flipped trees below or at \(\tau\) have been excluded by a valid bound or cutoff proof. It is open when a feasible flipped tree has score at most \(\tau\). It is unsettled when neither conclusion has been established. The solver pads the strict cutoff for numerical tolerances. A time limit can leave a useful bound or a feasible rival. A contradicted bound loses its evidential value.
Proposition 2 (competitive-set interpretation). A decided grouping agrees with \(T\) in every member of \(\Sset_m\). For an open grouping, disagreement in \(\Sset_m\) follows if both \(T\) and its rival \(F_M\) have scores at most \(L+m\), where \(L\le H^*\) is a valid lower bound on the same cumulative pool with no anchors required. An optimal reference supplies the special case \(L=H(T)=H^*\).

A feasible rival within \(\tau\) always establishes disagreement relative to the reference threshold. A bound from an anchored or smaller master pertains to its own domain. Applying it to the full current pool would require a separate validity argument. Labels are recomputed when the pool, reference or data change.

Local flips. Removing the lines of grouping \(M\) from \(T\), or swapping in a pool line \(D\) whose grouping crosses \(M\) (the lines of \(T\) that cross \(D\) removed), gives flipped trees without solving an MIQP. With \(T_{|D}=\{E\in T:\neg(E\cross D)\}\):

\[ m_M(T)=\min\Big\{H(T\setminus\pi^{-1}(M)),\ \min_{D:\ \pi(D)\cross M}H(T_{|D}\cup\{D\})\Big\}-H(T),\qquad \ell_D(T)=H(T)-H(T_{|D}\cup\{D\}) \]
(8)
Lemma 1 (local flips give upper bounds only). \(e_M(T)\le m_M(T)\) for \(M\in\Gset(T)\), and \(e_{\pi(D)}(T)\le-\ell_D(T)\) for \(\pi(D)\notin\Gset(T)\). Local flips can prove a grouping open, never decided.

Signal separation on a fixed domain. Let the data follow (1) with the true tree \(T^0\) and coefficients inside the bounds. After \(M\), write the data as \(\tilde y=\mu+\varepsilon\): \(\mu\) is the true voltage signal and \(\varepsilon\) the noise. For a tree \(F\), let \(P_F\) project onto everything \(F\) can predict (the span of its columns), and let \(d_F=\|\mu-P_F\mu\|\): the part of the true signal that \(F\) cannot reproduce with any coefficients. \(\mathrm{RSS}(F)\) is the residual of \(F\)'s bounded fit, the numerator of (4).

Theorem 1 (residual separation). (a) If \(F\) lacks a line of \(T^0\), then, with \(\xi_F=\langle\mu-P_F\mu,\ \varepsilon\rangle/d_F\) for \(d_F\gt0\) and \(\xi_F=0\) otherwise,
\[ \mathrm{RSS}(F)-\mathrm{RSS}(T^0)\ \ge\ d_F^2-2\,d_F\,|\xi_F|-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2 . \]
(b) If \(F\) contains every line of \(T^0\) and the unbounded least-squares fit of \(T^0\) lies within the bounds, then \(0\le\mathrm{RSS}(T^0)-\mathrm{RSS}(F)\le\|(P_F-P_{T^0})\varepsilon\|^2\).

The first bound compares the part of the signal missed by \(F\) with noise in the same measurements. When loads on two sides of a branch vary together, the missing line can yield very little separation. For a tree containing the true lines, the second bound limits its residual gain to the realised noise energy projected onto the additional directions. These are inequalities for a given data set and candidate family.

Corollary 1 (sufficient conditions in a fixed pool). Suppose the pool contains \(T^0\), the unbounded least-squares fit of \(T^0\) lies within the bounds, and (A) every tree \(F\) of the pool that lacks a line of \(T^0\) has \(\big(d_F^2-2d_F|\xi_F|-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2\big)/(2\hat\sigma^2)\gt m+\beta\,\big(k(T^0)-k(F)\big)\), and (B) every tree \(F\supsetneq T^0\) of the pool has \(\|(P_F-P_{T^0})\varepsilon\|^2\lt2\hat\sigma^2\beta\,\big(k(F)-k(T^0)\big)\). Then \(T^0\) is the unique best tree over the pool; a flip test relative to \(T^0\) that proves exclusion decides its grouping. If every anchor node, including the root, has at most \(K\) units, Proposition 4 puts every support of \(T^0\) in the anchored domain, so \(T^0\) stays feasible.

Conditions (A) and (B) compare against all competing trees of the stated pool and use the unknown true signal and realised noise. A feeder run can check its candidate coverage when ground truth is available, count incorrect decisions, and report open or unsettled cases.

IV. Identification Implemented, development

Tree MIQP. For a pool \(\Omega\) of supports, with a binary \(b_C\) per support,

\[ \begin{aligned} \min_{b,r,x}\ &\frac{\|M(y-\Phi(r,x))\|^2}{2\hat\sigma^2}+\beta\sum_{C\in\Omega}b_C\\ \text{s.t.}\ \ &0\le r_C\le\bar r_Cb_C,\quad0\le x_C\le\bar x_Cb_C\quad(C\in\Omega),\qquad b_C+b_D\le1\quad(C\cross D),\\ &\textstyle\sum_{C\in\Omega,\,C\subsetneq W}b_C\le|W|-2\quad(W\in\Omega\cup\{\Nset^+\}),\qquad \sum_{C\in\Omega,\ \pi(C)=A}b_C\ge1\quad(A\in\Aset),\\ &\textstyle\sum_{C\ni i}r_C\le\bar R_i,\quad\sum_{C\ni i}x_C\le\bar X_i\quad(i\in\Nset;\ \text{optional}) \end{aligned} \]
(9)

The base lines \(\Bset\) are always included. The count inequality holds because a tree on \(|W|\) leaves has at most \(|W|-2\) optional lines strictly inside \(W\). The next inequality requires at least one support for each anchor. Optional response bounds on the path sums \(\bar R_i,\bar X_i\) preserve the sublevel \(H\le\ell\). In a master solve a feasible tree supplies \(\ell\), so the sublevel contains an optimum. In a flip test \(\ell=\tau\) can lie below every feasible rival. In that case the tightened solve can establish threshold exclusion, while any reported numerical lower bound must also be valid for the original flip problem. The code checks bounds against feasible trees, scales coefficients to their design columns, and records the solver status. Local search supplies a feasible warm start and can improve a returned incumbent. Its score is an upper bound on that solve's minimum.

Proposition 3 (the load's path needs no constraints). For a set of meters \(C\) write \(C^1=C\cup\{s\}\). Over \(\Nset^+\) the crossing constraints of (9) are exactly: (i) the crossings of the meter sets; (ii) one place: \(b_{C^1}+b_{D^1}\le1\) unless \(C\subseteq D\) or \(D\subseteq C\); (iii) a line above a line that carries the load carries it too: \(b_{C^1}+b_D\le1\) for meter sets \(C\subsetneq D\). So the load's path needs neither extra constraints nor products of binaries and coefficients.

Node pricing. Let \(v\) be an internal node of \(T\) with children \(A_1,\dots,A_{c_v}\) (supports or leaves).

Lemma 2 (node decomposition). A support \(C\notin T\) with at least two leaves is compatible with \(T\) if and only if \(C=\bigcup_{j\in S}A_j\) for a unique internal node \(v\) and a set \(S\) of its children with \(2\le|S|\lt c_v\).
\[ g_v(T)=\max_{S:\ 2\le|S|\lt c_v}H(T)-H\Big(T\cup\Big\{\textstyle\bigcup_{j\in S}A_j\Big\}\Big) \]
(10)

At a node with at most \(K\) children, fitting every admissible union checks all \(2^{c_v}-c_v-2\) additions. At a wider node a single MIQP selects children with binary variables \(z_a\) and refits every coefficient of \(T\). A product \(u_{ab}=rz_az_b\) can be represented using \(u_{ab}\ge0\), \(u_{ab}\le r\), \(u_{ab}\le\bar\theta z_a\), \(u_{ab}\le\bar\theta z_b\), and \(u_{ab}\ge r-\bar\theta(2-z_a-z_b)\). In crit units a validated lower bound on the augmented score of at least \(\mathrm{crit}(T)-10^{-7}\) excludes improvement beyond \(10^{-7}\) at that node. A feasible addition that beats this tolerance can enter the tree immediately. If even one node lacks sufficient evidence, single-line pricing remains incomplete. Simultaneous additions can improve a tree that passes every single-line check (Remark 1).

Remark 1. Four meters, fit term \(\tfrac12\|R_{\rm obs}-R\|_F^2\) and price \(0.6\); \(R_{\rm obs}\) has diagonal \(100\) and off-diagonal \(1\), except \(2\) for the pairs \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\). The star scores \(4/3\), the best single line \(1.4\), and the lines \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\) together \(1.2\).

Anchors.

Definition 2 (anchors and units). An anchor is a decided grouping of \(T\). For a laminar set of anchors \(\Aset\) and each \(A\in\Aset\cup\{\Nset\}\), the units \(\Kset_A\) are the largest anchors strictly inside \(A\) and the meters of \(A\) outside them; \(k_A=|\Kset_A|\). For \(K\ge2\) the anchored universe is the set of meter lines
\[ \Omega_K(\Aset)=\Aset\ \cup\bigcup_{A:\,k_A\le K}\Big\{\textstyle\bigcup_{B\in S}B:\ S\subseteq\Kset_A,\ 2\le|S|\le k_A-1\Big\}\ \cup\ \Omega_{\gt K}(\Aset) \]
(11)
with \(\Omega_{\gt K}(\Aset)\) the groupings of the pool compatible with \(\Aset\) whose smallest enclosing member of \(\Aset\cup\{\Nset\}\) has more than \(K\) units. With a load, \(\Omega^+_K(\Aset)\) is every line of \(\Omega_K(\Aset)\) with and without \(s\), the supports \(\{i,s\}\), and \(\Nset\); without a load, \(\Omega^+_K=\Omega_K\).
Proposition 4 (anchored universe). Let \(F\) be a tree over \(\Nset^+\) that keeps every anchor (\(\Aset\subseteq\Gset(F)\)). Every line \(C\) of \(F\) has \(\pi(C)\) equal to an anchor, to a union of 2 to \(k_A-1\) units of one \(A\in\Aset\cup\{\Nset\}\), to a single meter, or to \(\Nset\). Hence, if every \(k_A\le K\), (9) over \(\Omega^+_K(\Aset)\) with the anchors required, solved to optimality, returns the best tree that keeps every anchor, among all supports and every place of the load; it has \(\sum_A(2^{k_A}-k_A-2)\) union binaries (twice that with a load) instead of \(2^n-n-2\).

The anchored master optimises over the supports actually included and the groupings required in that round. Wide anchor nodes retain compatible candidates already in the pool. The next labelling step again tests the groupings on the cumulative pool without requiring anchors. It can change an earlier decision.

Pricing, re-solve and stopping. Every line added by pricing enters the cumulative pool, including a line that the subsequent master drops from its incumbent tree. When pricing adds lines, the method re-solves (9) on the expanded round domain with the same anchor requirements. Each master record identifies its candidate lines, required groups, incumbent score, valid available lower bound, solver status and gap tolerance. A restricted certificate refers to the latest actual master, including the post-pricing re-solve when present.

Lemma 3 (stopping scope). With exact optimisation and exact pricing, if the anchors stay unchanged, the restricted master is solved to optimality, and pricing the incumbent adds no line and excludes every improving single addition, repeating that round cannot improve the score. The implementation checks a master gap at most \(\max(10^{-7},10^{-4}|\mathrm{crit}(T)|)\) and single-line improvement at most \(10^{-7}\) in crit units. A stop at the round limit is reported separately.

Load evidence. Whether a load is modelled is decided by the gate (3). The fit reports, as a diagnostic, how strongly the voltages show the load: with \(H_0\) the score of the same data and \(\hat\sigma^2\) with \(u\equiv0\), minimised over meter topologies, and \(H\) minimised with the load,

\[ D^*=\min H_0-\min H,\qquad L_0-U_1\ \le\ D^*\ \le\ U_0-L_1 \]
(12)

where \([L_0,U_0]\) and \([L_1,U_1]\) bracket the two minima (solver bounds and incumbents): \(\min H_0\ge L_0\) and \(\min H\le U_1\) give the lower end, and likewise the upper. Both brackets come from the tree MIQP over the final pool (over its meter groupings for \(H_0\)), started from \(\hat T\); \(U_1\) is the better of that solve and \(H(\hat T)\). Evaluating \(H_0\) at the load tree's own meter topology instead could only overstate \(D^*\), since that topology is one of those minimised over. The places of the load are the admissible places in \(\hat T\)'s meter topology whose score is within \(m\) of the best.

Explanation

The nine builders affect candidate coverage and the feasible starting score. Local fitting can find rivals cheaply and improve a warm start. A label of exclusion comes from a flip bound or cutoff proof over the cumulative pool. The anchored master and single-line pricing answer two narrower questions, so the output keeps their scopes and stopping reason. Groupings inside the final tree are retested. In identification, outside groupings are reported open when a feasible local swap reaches the threshold. Other outside groupings can remain untested.

V. Probing Simulated days only

The operator can buy signed active-power blocks at chosen meters from an aggregator of household batteries and EV chargers, each held for one market hour and sealed one hour ahead. Probing works on the pool, the leader and the open alternatives; it never lists whole trees. A block that changes the flows on one side of a branching point makes the leader's and the rival's predictions differ more: this is what probing buys.

Values used in probing

For an open alternative \(M\) with rival \(F_M\), at the gate of hour \(h\):

  • the gap interval (7) and the deficit \(\underline\delta_M=m-\bar e_M\) with \(\bar e_M=U_M-H(\hat L)\ge e_M\): a lower estimate of what the alternative still needs; unsettled groupings are not targets (probing cannot repair a solver's time limit) and are labelled again at each gate;
  • the fitted parameters of the leader and the rival and their covariances \(P=\hat\sigma^2(\Phi\tp\Phi)^{-1}\) on the coordinates not at a bound;
  • the predicted voltage drops of hour \(h+1\) under each tree after \(M\), so that \(\hat\sigma^2I\) is their noise covariance, from the day-ahead forecast of the meters' load (the mean of the same hour over the data) plus the block \(u\); their difference is \(d_M(u)=d_M(0)+G_Mu\), and
    \[ q_M(u)=d_M(u)\tp\Sigma_M^{-1}d_M(u),\qquad \Sigma_M=\hat\sigma^2I+\tfrac12\Phi_{\hat L}P_{\hat L}\Phi_{\hat L}\tp+\tfrac12\Phi_{F_M}P_{F_M}\Phi_{F_M}\tp \]
    (13)
    with \(\Sigma_M\) held fixed at the forecast;
  • the feasible blocks \(\Uset_{h+1}\) (battery and EV headroom, the voltage band for the leader and every rival, the transformer rating, the price caps) and the price \(c(u)=c^\uparrow\sum_i\max(u_i,0)+c^\downarrow\sum_i\max(-u_i,0)\). A block acts at one meter with one sign, at a quarter, a half, three quarters or all of the largest size these allow; the band keeps \(z\hat\sigma/2\) of margin (one reading of \(V\)).
Lemma 4 (expected predictive log-likelihood ratio). With the two fitted predictive means \(\mu_{\hat L}(u)\), \(\mu_{F_M}(u)\) and the covariance \(\Sigma_M\) held fixed, let
\[ \ell_M(Y;u)=\ln\frac{\mathcal N\big(Y;\mu_{\hat L}(u),\Sigma_M\big)}{\mathcal N\big(Y;\mu_{F_M}(u),\Sigma_M\big)} . \]
(14)
If the next hour's data are \(\mathcal N(\mu_{\hat L},\Sigma_M)\), then \(\ell_M\sim\mathcal N(\tfrac12q_M,q_M)\).

The quantity \(q_M/2\) is a conditional forecast under the stated Gaussian model for the next hour. The identification score refits both trees and updates its penalty with \(N\), so an observed score change can differ from this forecast.

Lemma 5 (reach). If \(G_Mv=0\) for every feasible block direction \(v\), no feasible block changes \(q_M\).

\(G_M\) must be computed from the two fitted trees, not from their structure alone. For example, two trees that differ only in whether the line \(\{1,2\}\) carries the load can fit the history equally when the load's profile equals \(p_1+p_2\) (the first with \(r=2\) and no load, the second with \(r=1\) and the load); a block \(a\) at meter 1 then gives \(2a\) against \(a\), so a block does separate them.

Target set and objective. At the start of the day, \(\Tset\) is the set of open groupings, and the load's place when the second-best admissible place is within \(m\) of the best (that place is its rival), whose \(G\) moves on some feasible block direction; it stays fixed during the day. At every gate each target is relabelled on the current data and pool; a target outside the tree that no local swap shows open gets the flip test (6) with its grouping required, since a local swap cannot decide it (Lemma 1); its best swap is the known tree that the test's claim is checked against. With \(\kappa(\delta,q)=\mathbb E\min(\delta,Z)\) for \(Z\sim\mathcal N(q/2,q)\), that is \(\kappa=\tfrac q2-(\tfrac q2-\delta)\,\mathrm F_0\big(\tfrac{q/2-\delta}{\sqrt q}\big)-\sqrt q\,\mathrm f_0\big(\tfrac{q/2-\delta}{\sqrt q}\big)\) with \(\mathrm F_0\) and \(\mathrm f_0\) the standard normal distribution and density (\(\kappa=\min(\delta,0)\) at \(q=0\)), the block for hour \(h+1\) is

\[ u^\ast=\arg\max_{u\in\Uset_{h+1}} J_h(u),\qquad J_h(u)=\psi\sum_{M\in\Tset\ \text{open}}\Big[\kappa\big(\underline\delta_M,q_M(u)\big)-\kappa\big(\underline\delta_M,q_M(0)\big)\Big]-c(u) \]
(15)

The rule buys a block when \(J_h(u^\ast)\gt0\). Its value compares the expected capped predictive log-likelihood ratio with passive operation and subtracts the purchase price. It holds the two fitted models and covariance fixed and assumes the leader generates the next observation. At \(q=2\), \(\delta=1\), capping the mean would give \(1\), whereas the expected capped value is \(0.436\). The simulation used a placeholder \(\psi=5\). Selecting \(\psi\) from a cost–coverage curve and assessing it against passive operation are development tasks. Each purchase considers one hour and requires \(s^2_{\hat L}/\sigma^2_y\le\kappa_A\), where \(s^2_{\hat L}\) is the leader's residual variance and \(\sigma^2_y=4\sigma_v^2\,1.02^4\) for the meter class \(\sigma_v=0.05\%\). Stopping requires fresh decided labels at two consecutive gates. A final gate uses the last block's readings.

VI. How the outputs are valued

Reading the output. The load record gives the gate statistic, and, when a load is included, its path, admissible places within the margin and the score evidence interval. The structure record gives the reference tree, its decided anchors, labels for the groupings tested, available gap endpoints and open rivals. The computation record identifies the actual master domain and its bounds, the single-line pricing outcome and the stopping reason. A grouping can remain unsettled after a time limit.

How it is scored against the truth. The gate: loads missed and no-load feeders given a load, by share and kind. The structure: precision, recall and F1 over groupings; recall near the root; exact trees; false anchors; label coverage, false decisions, the share of open groupings that are true, and the share unsettled under each time limit. The load: whether its true place is within \(m\) of the best. Probing: the share of targets decided by the end of the day, false decisions against passive operation, cost, energy and time; over \(\psi\), as a cost–coverage curve. Coverage and false decisions are always reported together.

VII. Contributions, limits and tests

Contributions in v17. The method turns a feeder-topology fit into a set of checkable statements about individual meter groupings. A grouping flip treats both possible placements of an unmetered load consistently and either exhibits a feasible rival, excludes all rivals within the threshold, or leaves the result unsettled. The domain and bounds identify how far each statement extends. Decided groupings generate smaller anchored searches, and exhaustive or bounded pricing checks compatible single-line additions. The residual inequalities explain when a true grouping survives exclusion in a fixed candidate pool. Probing is a separate extension whose one-hour predictive objective still needs comparative evaluation.

Relation to earlier work. Bremer measured the additional tree length needed to lose a branch among near-most-parsimonious trees. Marx, Calmon and Ustun examined disagreement among near-optimal classifiers by minimising classification error subject to reversing one prediction. Pengwah and colleagues studied distribution topology with partial smart-meter coverage by estimating impedances and then placing meters. Here the fit uses measured powers and voltages. The test reverses a grouping of meters across admissible placements of an unmetered load and records which alternative feeder trees the search covered. Bremer (1994) · Marx et al. (2020) · Pengwah et al. (2024)

Limits. Missing supports in the cumulative pool can hide competitive trees. Equation (1) fits one estimated unmetered injection and approximates feeder physics. The score penalty, load gate and margin are design choices. The signal and noise conditions in Corollary 1 require knowledge of the true signal. Responses to changes in candidate pool and penalty, and false decisions against known topology, therefore need separate measurements.

The complete v16 EU LV run used real feeder topologies with simulated AC measurements on 66 feeder-day inputs. It classified 162 reference-tree groupings as decided, 192 as open and 29 as unsettled. Appendix A. Proofs

Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 analyse residual separation for a fixed model and pool. The propositions and lemmas specify which alternatives each optimisation or feasible witness covers. Lemma 4 additionally assumes a Gaussian predictive model for the next hour.

Proof of Theorem 1. Two facts about least squares. (F1) A tree that contains every line of \(T^0\) can use the true coefficients (and zero on its other lines), which leave the residual \(\tilde y-\mu=\varepsilon\); its best fit is no worse, so its RSS is at most \(\|\varepsilon\|^2\). (F2) No fit of \(F\), bounded or not, beats the unbounded least-squares fit, whose residual is \(Q_F\tilde y\) with \(Q_F=I-P_F\); so \(\mathrm{RSS}(F)\ge\|Q_F\tilde y\|^2\). (a) By (F1) for \(T^0\) and (F2) for \(F\), \(\mathrm{RSS}(F)-\mathrm{RSS}(T^0)\ge\|Q_F(\mu+\varepsilon)\|^2-\|\varepsilon\|^2\). Expanding the square, and using \(\|Q_F\varepsilon\|^2=\|\varepsilon\|^2-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2\) (Pythagoras),
\[ \|Q_F(\mu+\varepsilon)\|^2-\|\varepsilon\|^2=\|Q_F\mu\|^2+2\langle Q_F\mu,\varepsilon\rangle-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2=d_F^2+2d_F\xi_F-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2\ \ge\ d_F^2-2d_F|\xi_F|-\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2 , \]
since \(Q_F\) is symmetric and \(Q_F^2=Q_F\), so that \(\langle Q_F\mu,Q_F\varepsilon\rangle=\langle Q_F\mu,\varepsilon\rangle\). (b) \(F\) can reproduce any fit of \(T^0\) by setting its extra lines' coefficients to zero, so the difference is at least \(0\). The unbounded fit of \(T^0\) lies within the bounds, so it is also its bounded fit; \(\mu\) lies in the span of \(T^0\)'s columns, so \(\mathrm{RSS}(T^0)=\|Q_{T^0}\tilde y\|^2=\|Q_{T^0}\varepsilon\|^2\). \(\mu\) lies in the span of \(F\)'s columns too, so (F2) gives \(\mathrm{RSS}(F)\ge\|Q_F\varepsilon\|^2\). Subtracting, and using Pythagoras twice, \(\|Q_{T^0}\varepsilon\|^2-\|Q_F\varepsilon\|^2=\|P_F\varepsilon\|^2-\|P_{T^0}\varepsilon\|^2=\|(P_F-P_{T^0})\varepsilon\|^2\): the span of \(T^0\)'s columns lies in that of \(F\)'s, so \(P_F-P_{T^0}\) projects onto the part of \(F\)'s span orthogonal to \(T^0\)'s. ∎
Proof of Corollary 1. The score is \(H=\mathrm{RSS}/(2\hat\sigma^2)+\beta k\). Take a tree \(F\ne T^0\) of the pool. If \(F\) lacks a line of \(T^0\), Theorem 1(a) and (A) give \(H(F)-H(T^0)\gt m\). Otherwise \(F\) contains every line of \(T^0\) and \(k(F)-k(T^0)\ge1\) more, and Theorem 1(b) and (B) give \(H(T^0)-H(F)\le\|(P_F-P_{T^0})\varepsilon\|^2/(2\hat\sigma^2)-\beta\,\big(k(F)-k(T^0)\big)\lt0\). So \(T^0\) is the unique best tree. Every tree that flips a grouping of \(T^0\) lacks the line of that grouping, so it scores more than \(H(T^0)+m\). A valid exclusion proof at this threshold establishes the label. Every resulting anchor is present in \(T^0\). If all anchor nodes have at most \(K\) units, Proposition 4 includes all its supports, hence \(T^0\) remains feasible there. ∎
Proof of Proposition 1. A tree with \(n^+\) leaves (the load counts as one) and \(k+1\) internal nodes has \(n^++k\) edges, one from each node except the root to its parent, so \(\sum_vc_v=n^++k\) and \(\sum_v(c_v-2)=n^++k-2(k+1)=n^+-k-2\). Hence \(w(F)=a^{n^+-2}a^{-k}\), and \(-\ln w(F)=\mathrm{const}-k\ln(1/a)\). ∎
Proof of Proposition 2. Since \(T\) is feasible, \(H^*\le H(T)\). Excluding all rivals through \(H(T)+m\) therefore excludes all rivals through \(H^*+m\). For disagreement, a bound \(L\le H^*\) gives \(L+m\le H^*+m\). If both feasible witnesses score at most \(L+m\), both belong to \(\Sset_m\) and their groupings differ. ∎
Proof of Lemma 1. Each tree in (8) is laminar and decides the grouping the other way (a swapped-in \(D\) whose meters cross \(M\) removes every line of grouping \(M\), since those lines cross \(D\)), so it is a feasible point of (6), and the minimum is at most its score. A feasible point bounds a minimum from above, never from below. ∎
Proof of Proposition 3. \(C^1\) and \(D^1\) both contain \(s\), so they are never disjoint: they are compatible exactly when one contains the other, that is when \(C\subseteq D\) or \(D\subseteq C\). For \(C^1\) and \(D\) (without \(s\)): \(C^1\subseteq D\) is impossible, \(D\subseteq C^1\) holds exactly when \(D\subseteq C\), and they are disjoint exactly when \(C\cap D=\emptyset\); so they cross exactly when \(C\subsetneq D\) or \(C\) and \(D\) cross. Supports without \(s\) cross as meter sets. ∎
Proof of Lemma 2. If \(C\) is a union of 2 to \(c_v-1\) children of \(v\), it is nested with or disjoint from every support of \(T\), and equals none. Conversely, let \(v\) be the smallest support of \(T\) containing \(C\). No child of \(v\) contains \(C\), so each child is inside \(C\) or disjoint from it; the children partition \(v\), so \(C\) is a union of children, at least two and not all. ∎
Proof of Proposition 4. Removing \(s\) keeps inclusions and disjointness, so \(\pi(C)\) is compatible with every anchor. If \(2\le|\pi(C)|\le n-1\), let \(A\) be the smallest member of \(\Aset\cup\{\Nset\}\) containing \(\pi(C)\). Each unit of \(A\) is inside \(\pi(C)\) or disjoint from it: a meter trivially, and an anchor unit \(B\) because \(\pi(C)\subsetneq B\) would contradict the choice of \(A\). So \(\pi(C)\) is a union of units of \(A\): one unit makes it an anchor, all of them make it \(A\). Otherwise \(\pi(C)\) is a single meter (\(C\) is \(\{i,s\}\)) or \(\Nset\). In every case \(C\) is \(\pi(C)\) or \(\pi(C)\cup\{s\}\), which is in \(\Omega^+_K(\Aset)\) when every \(k_A\le K\). A node with \(k\) units has \(2^k-k-2\) unions of 2 to \(k-1\) units. ∎
Proof of Lemma 3. An exact master minimises over its recorded domain and retains the feasible incumbent. If the anchors stay unchanged and no addition was made, repeating the anchored construction adds no new supports to that domain. The previous minimiser remains feasible and its certified single-line check excludes an improving addition to that tree. This proves the stated scope for exact solves. ∎
Proof of Lemma 4. Both densities have covariance \(\Sigma_M\), so the terms quadratic in \(Y\) cancel and, with \(d=\mu_{\hat L}-\mu_{F_M}\), \(\ell_M=d\tp\Sigma_M^{-1}\big(Y-\tfrac12(\mu_{\hat L}+\mu_{F_M})\big)\). If \(Y\sim\mathcal N(\mu_{\hat L},\Sigma_M)\), the bracket has mean \(\tfrac12d\) and covariance \(\Sigma_M\), so \(\ell_M\) is Gaussian with mean \(\tfrac12d\tp\Sigma_M^{-1}d=\tfrac12q_M\) and variance \(d\tp\Sigma_M^{-1}\Sigma_M\Sigma_M^{-1}d=q_M\). ∎
Proof of Lemma 5. \(d_M(u)=d_M(0)+G_Mu\) and \(\Sigma_M\) is fixed, so \(q_M(u)\) depends on \(u\) only through \(G_Mu\), which is zero for every feasible block. ∎

Any tree builders can supply \(\Omega_0\); this work uses nine. (1) A sensitivity matrix over the meters without tree structure, fitted to the active-power part of (1) by a convex QP solved with Gurobi. (B1)

with \(\epsilon\) a strict order margin, \(10^{-6}\) of the median diagonal of the unconstrained fit.

(2) Nine hierarchies over the meters: recursive neighbour joining, neighbour joining (with the PCC as an observed leaf) and recursive grouping (equalities to \(10^{-9}\) of the median depth) on the shared paths \(\hat R/\bar d\), \(\bar d\) the mean positive path distance; UPGMA on the path distance \(d_{ij}=\hat R_{ii}+\hat R_{jj}-2\hat R_{ij}\) and Ward linkage on \(\sqrt d\); average-linkage clusterings on the correlation distance between meters of the rows of \(\hat R\), the drops \(y\), the drops \((D_1\otimes S_\gamma)y\), and the residuals after fitting the service cables and the trunk alone.
